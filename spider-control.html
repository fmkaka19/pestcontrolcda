Safety & Identification

Are the Spiders in Your Home Dangerous?

Most are not. Spiders eat other insects, and the ones you find in North Idaho homes are usually harmless. A few are worth knowing about.

Take Seriously

Western black widow

Shiny black with a red hourglass on the belly. Black widows build messy, tangled webs close to the ground in woodpiles, sheds, garages, meter boxes, and under decks. A bite can cause strong pain, cramping, sweating, and nausea. It’s rarely deadly but needs medical care, especially for children, older adults, and anyone with health problems. Wear gloves when moving wood or stored items.

Caution

Hobo spiders

Common in the Pacific Northwest. They are brown with a funnel-shaped web, often near the ground, in window wells, and in basements. Their bites were once blamed for serious skin damage. Most researchers now doubt that, but we still treat them as a spider you don’t want to handle.

Mild Sting

Yellow sac spiders

Pale yellow, no web, and often found on walls and ceilings at night. They can bite, usually causing mild pain and redness similar to a sting.

Not in Idaho

Brown recluse

People often fear these, but they are not established in Idaho. Most “recluse” bites here turn out to be something else, like another insect or a skin infection. If you have a worrying bite, see a doctor.

Harmless

Common Household Spiders

Wolf spiders, cellar spiders, jumping spiders, orb weavers, and common house spiders. These are harmless to people. They can look scary, and wolf spiders especially can startle you indoors, but they rarely bite.

Nuisance Impact

What spiders really cost you

Mostly, they are a nuisance. Webs, egg sacs, and the stress of finding them can be a problem, especially in kids’ rooms, garages, and vacation rentals or businesses where customers notice.

If someone is bitten:

Wash the area, apply a cold pack, and watch for worsening pain, spreading redness, cramping, or fever. If you think it was a black widow, or symptoms are strong, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or seek medical care. If you can safely catch the spider, bring it or a photo.

Regional Factors

Why Spiders Move In Around Coeur d’Alene

Spiders follow their food. If you have bugs, you’ll have spiders. Here is what draws them in North Idaho:

Lots of insects

Lakes, trees, and gardens mean plenty of flies, gnats, moths, and beetles for spiders to eat.

Porch and exterior lights

Lights attract nocturnal insects, and spiders set up webs directly near them to catch prey.

Woodpiles and yard clutter

Firewood, stacked lumber, and old storage boxes make ideal undisturbed hiding places for black widows and hobo spiders.

Garages, sheds, and crawl spaces

They are dark, quiet, and rarely disturbed, offering undisturbed harboring for funnel webs and egg sacs.

Shrubs and trees touching the house

Branches work like direct aerial bridges to your siding, windows, gutters, and roof edges.

Cracks and gaps

Spiders get in through gaps around doors, windows, utility pipes, dryer vents, and foundation edges.

Lake and waterfront homes

Lake Coeur d'Alene and Hayden Lake bring more insects, more vegetation, and more places to hide around decks and docks.

Cooler weather

In late summer and fall, spiders move toward buildings looking for shelter, and wandering males show up indoors more often.

Post-Care Instructions

What To Expect After Treatment

You may still see spiders for a couple of weeks. Spiders die after walking across treated areas, and egg sacs may hatch. This is normal.
Don’t wipe down treated areas. Leave baseboards, corners, and exterior treated spots alone until we say it’s okay.
Knock down webs lightly. Remove new webs so you can see what’s being rebuilt, but avoid heavy cleaning where we treated.
Expect a drop over days to weeks. Heavy infestations or lots of egg sacs can take longer to fully decline.
Call us if spiders remain active. Call us if spiders are still active after the time window we give you.

Homeowner Warnings

Common Mistakes To Avoid

Don’t just sweep away webs. Spiders usually rebuild within days. Webs tell you where to look and where active spiders hide.
Don’t spray insecticide on webs and call it done. Spiders often hide deep in cracks and voids where surface spray never reaches.
Don’t handle woodpiles bare-handed. Black widows hide there. Always wear gloves and sturdy shoes when moving wood or boxes.
Don’t leave porch lights on all night. They draw the flying insects that spiders eat. Switch to yellow bug bulbs or motion sensors.
Don’t ignore egg sacs. One sac can hold dozens to hundreds of spiderlings ready to spread through the home.
Don’t stack firewood against the house. Keep wood stacks elevated and several feet away from siding and foundation perimeters.

Proactive Maintenance

Spider Prevention Checklist

Do these yourself to cut off spider food, shelter, and access points:

Knock down webs and egg sacs. From windows, eaves, garages, and sheds. A vacuum with a hose works well.
Seal structural gaps. Seal gaps around doors, windows, pipes, vents, and utility lines.
Check door sweeps and window screens. Replace worn door sweeps and torn window screens promptly.
Switch exterior lighting. Switch to yellow “bug” bulbs or motion lights outside, and turn off lights you don’t need.
Trim branches and shrubs. Ensure branches and vegetation do not touch the house, roof, or deck.
Move firewood and lumber away. Move firewood and lumber at least a few feet from the house and elevate it off the ground.
Clean out clutter. In garages, sheds, and crawl spaces. Use sealed plastic bins instead of cardboard.
Wear gloves when moving items. Wear gloves when you move stored items, boxes, or stacked wood.
Clear perimeter debris. Clear leaves and debris from window wells, foundations, and under decks.
Reduce insects. Keep food sealed, fix damp spots, empty trash, and clean up pet food.
Seasonal Lifecycle

Spider Activity Through the Year in North Idaho

Understanding how spider behaviors shift each season helps you protect your home year-round.

Spring

Waking Up & Hatching

Spiders wake up and start hunting as insects return. Egg sacs hatch, and tiny spiders spread across siding and gardens.

Summer

Peak Webs & Insects

Expect more spiders around porches, eaves, gardens, and exterior lights, and more webs on decks and windows.

Fall

Seeking Shelter & Mates

Spiders seek shelter and mates before the freeze. Wandering male spiders, including hobo spiders, often show up indoors. Garages and basements get busy.

Winter

Sheltered Inactivity

Many spiders slow down, but some stay active in warm, sheltered spots like garages, basements, crawl spaces, and storage areas.

Help & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct, expert answers about spider control in Coeur d'Alene.

Ask a Specialist: (208) 248-2701
How much does spider control cost in Coeur d’Alene?

It depends on property size, how many spiders you have, where they are, and what’s drawing them in. We give you a written estimate before work starts. Call (208) 248-2701.

Why do I keep getting spiders in my house?

Usually they’re following insects, or they’re getting in through gaps. Lights, plants against the house, clutter, and wood piles make it worse. Sweeping webs alone won’t fix it.

How do I get rid of spiders in my home?

We inspect, identify the spider, remove webs and egg sacs where we can, and treat the hiding spots and entry areas. We also help with the insects feeding them. Fixing gaps, lighting, and clutter helps results last.

Are spiders common around Coeur d’Alene homes?

Yes. North Idaho has lots of insects, trees, and sheltered spots. Most spiders are harmless, but a few, like the western black widow, are worth taking seriously.

How can I prevent spiders from entering my home?

Seal gaps, trim plants off the house, reduce outdoor lighting, move woodpiles away, clear out clutter, and remove webs and egg sacs regularly.

Why are there so many spider webs around my house?

Webs usually mean there are plenty of insects, lights, or sheltered spots nearby. Eaves, windows, porches, and garages are the most common places.

Are all spiders dangerous?

No. Most are harmless. The western black widow is the main one in our area that can cause a serious bite. Hobo and yellow sac spiders can bite but rarely cause major problems. Brown recluses are not established in Idaho.

What should I do if I find a black widow?

Don’t handle it. Keep kids and pets away and call us. If someone is bitten, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or seek medical care.

Do spiders come back after treatment?

They can. New spiders can arrive from outside, and egg sacs may hatch. That’s why we also look at lights, bugs, gaps, and clutter.

Is treatment safe for kids and pets?

We choose products and placement carefully and tell you what to do after, like keeping pets away from treated areas until dry.

Do I need to be home?

We typically recommend being present for the initial interior inspection so we can discuss problem areas, while exterior perimeter treatments around eaves, windows, and exterior walls can often be handled flexibly. Call (208) 248-2701 to coordinate your schedule.

When is the best time to treat spiders?

Any time you see repeated activity. Late summer and fall are especially busy as spiders move toward buildings, so a fall treatment can help, and spring is a good time to get ahead of egg hatching.
