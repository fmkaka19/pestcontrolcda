Health & Property Impact

Are Bed Bugs Dangerous?

Bed bugs are not known to spread disease. They are still a serious problem, and the longer they stay, the harder they are to remove.

Itchy bites

Bites show up as red, itchy welts on the face, neck, arms, and hands, often in a line or cluster. Scratching can lead to infection.

Different reactions

Some people get large welts, and some show no reaction at all. Bites alone can’t confirm bed bugs.

Lost sleep and stress

Many people struggle to sleep, feel anxious, or feel ashamed. Bed bugs are not a sign of poor hygiene, and they can affect anyone.

Fast spread

Bed bugs move between rooms, furniture, and neighboring apartments. They can also sit unnoticed for months before you find them.

Hard to beat alone

They hide in tiny cracks, and store-bought sprays and foggers often scatter them into new rooms.

Business and rental impact

Bed bugs can mean guest complaints, bad reviews, lost bookings, and tenant disputes.

Medical notice:

If you have signs of infection, such as spreading redness, pus, or fever, see a doctor.

Visual Identification

Do You Have Bed Bugs?

Adult bed bugs are flat, reddish-brown, and about the size of an apple seed. They turn purplish-red after feeding.

  • Live bugs in mattress seams, box springs, headboards, and furniture joints
  • Rusty or dark red spots on sheets and mattresses
  • Dark dots that look like pen marks (droppings)
  • Shed skins left after molting
  • Tiny, pale young bugs, as small as a pinhead
  • Small white eggs in seams and cracks
  • A musty, sweet odor in heavy infestations
  • Bites in a line or cluster on exposed skin
Accurate ID Comes First

Many Things Get Mistaken for Bed Bugs

Mosquito and other insect bites look similar, and similar bugs infest bats, swallows, and poultry. Fleas also bite and leave itchy marks. Researchers have found that most samples sent in for identification were not bed bugs. Getting a correct ID comes first.

Not sure? Take a clear photo or keep the bug in a sealed container, and show us. We confirm bed bugs before we treat.

Post-Care Instructions

What To Expect After Treatment

You may still see a few bed bugs for a couple of weeks. Eggs hatch after treatment, and new bugs die after touching treated areas.
Expect more than one visit. Most jobs need follow-ups to catch newly hatched bugs.
Use interceptor traps under bed legs. They show whether bugs are still active and moving toward the bed.
Keep encasements on for at least a full year. The EPA recommends this, because bed bugs can live a long time without feeding.
Don’t wipe away treated areas. Don’t wipe away treated areas until we say it’s okay.
Keep washing and vacuuming as we advise. Regular maintenance accelerates eradication and removes dead insects.
Call us if you still see bugs or get new bites. Reach out if activity persists beyond the time we give you.

Proactive Protection

Bed Bug Prevention Checklist

Simple habits to protect your home and prevent bed bug hitchhikers:

Check hotel rooms when you travel. Look at headboards, mattress seams, baseboards, and picture frames before you bring bags in.
Keep luggage off the bed and floor. Use a luggage rack, or keep bags in the bathroom.
Wash travel clothes right away. Wash on high heat immediately when you get home from trips.
Store luggage safely. Store luggage in sealed plastic bags, away from bedrooms.
Check used furniture. Check used furniture before bringing it in. Never bring in a discarded mattress or couch.
Use zippered encasements. Use zippered encasements on your mattress and box spring.

Inspect short-term rentals. For short-term rentals, inspect beds and furniture after every guest checkout.
Seal seasonal cabin linens. Seal cabin linens in plastic tubs when you close up for the season.

Bed Bug FAQs

Direct, practical answers about bed bug inspection and treatment in Coeur d'Alene.

Ask a Specialist: (208) 248-2701
How much does bed bug treatment cost in Coeur d’Alene?

It depends on property size, how bad the infestation is, the treatment method, and how many follow-up visits are needed. We give you a free inspection and a written estimate before work starts. Call (208) 248-2701.

How do I know if I have bed bugs in my Coeur d’Alene home?

Look for live bugs in mattress seams, rusty spots on sheets, dark dots (droppings), shed skins, and bites in a line or cluster. Bites alone aren’t proof, so we confirm before we treat.

What do bed bugs look like?

Adults are flat, reddish-brown, and about the size of an apple seed. After feeding, they look purplish-red. Young bugs can be as small as a pinhead and pale.

Are bed bugs a sign of a dirty home?

No. Bed bugs follow people and can affect any home, hotel, or rental.

Can bed bugs spread disease?

They are not known to spread disease. Bites can itch and may get infected if scratched.

Where do bed bugs hide?

In mattress seams, box springs, bed frames, headboards, baseboards, furniture joints, electrical outlets, and behind picture frames.

How do bed bugs get into my home?

They hitchhike on luggage, clothing, bedding, and used furniture. In apartments, they can also travel through shared walls and conduits.

Can I get rid of bed bugs myself?

Washing, vacuuming, and encasements help, but they rarely solve the problem alone. Store-bought sprays and foggers often scatter bed bugs. A licensed inspection finds where they hide.

Should I throw out my mattress?

No. Throwing out a mattress can spread bed bugs and rarely solves the problem. Keep it and use a zippered encasement.

Why do I still see bed bugs after treatment?

Eggs hatch after treatment, and new bugs die after touching treated areas. That’s why follow-up visits are common.

How long does it take to get rid of bed bugs?

Most jobs need more than one visit. We tell you what to expect after the inspection.

Are bed bugs the same as fleas?

No. Fleas jump and are often tied to pets. Bed bugs crawl and hide in beds and furniture. Both bite, so we confirm which one you have.

How can I prevent bed bugs?

Check hotel rooms, keep luggage off the bed and floor, wash travel clothes on high heat, check used furniture, and use mattress encasements.

When should I call?

As soon as you find bugs, rusty spots, or unexplained bites. Early treatment is simpler.
